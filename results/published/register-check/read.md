## Thoughts

Reported beside the lines, not part of the decision rule.

**1.** She asks whose ledger I keep — a fair question from a woman I dare not look at too long, and a dangerous one to fumble. I'll answer straight as a soldier: France's ledger, and France pays honest men who keep their word. But I'll turn it lightly toward heraldry — a boar, a crest — as idle wonder, and watch Raffaello and Francesco for who stiffens. My story stays straight: justice for a man seven years dead, no brother named, no seal shown.

**2.** Francesco wants me to name my debt in front of LeNoir and Lupo, and he's calling it 'Medici silver buying tonight' to make it sound like bribery and scheming. He has no proof — he's guessing and fishing, and if I name the Contessa and the sum, he'll have his weapon. I'll answer him level: my debts are my own, settled with the men I owe, and I'll not be made a traitor by his guessing. My story stays straight — no French pension, debts my own.

**3.** She named the Salviati and then asked why — a careful woman testing why a French captain cares about a boar. I'll give her a soldier's plain reason and press her on who else bears the boar, and watch whether she grows uneasy. Giorgia stands silent beside her, and I keep my eyes where they belong.

**4.** He demands a deed — a trap dressed as a challenge. Whatever I name, he will answer that the Medici did it twice and better, and I will be left defending our record while he sits smug.
